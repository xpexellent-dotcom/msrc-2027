CONSISTENT SCORING

How to assign points

Use the common 1-5 scale, then consult the criterion-specific anchors below. Rate the proposal at its current concept stage.

5 Excellent            Convincing, well-supported; no material deficiency.
4 Strong               Well addressed, with minor gaps.
3 Adequate             Core expectations met; substantive gaps remain.
2 Weak                 Major deficiencies reduce confidence.
1 Poor                 Criterion largely unclear, unsupported or unaddressed.

CRITERION-SPECIFIC ANCHORS                                                                         EXAMPLES FOR SCORES 5 / 3 / 1
01 Problem & Need                                   1: vague or unjustified need
                                                    1: unsupported or misinterpreted claims
   5: clear, significant need, users and causes     1: poor fit or negligible added value
   3: relevant but incomplete context               1: unrealistic or major barriers ignored
                                                    1: overstated or unclear likely benefit
02 Evidence & Scientific Rationale                  1: unclear or substantially disorganized
                                                    1: inaccurate, evasive or poorly understood
   5: credible support and justified mechanism
   3: some support but important gaps

03 Innovation & Solution Fit

   5: meaningful advancement with strong fit
   3: reasonable fit, limited differentiation

04 Feasibility & Implementation

   5: realistic pathway, barriers and safeguards
   3: plausible but incomplete plan

05 Impact & Sustainability

   5: credible benefits, measures and continuation
   3: plausible but weak measurement or plan

06 Pitch & Communication

   5: concise, coherent and easy to follow
   3: understandable but uneven or crowded

07 Q&A & Defense

   5: accurate, reasoned answers; honest limits
   3: adequate with significant knowledge gaps

Fairness rule: Evaluate conceptual merit, not slide design, charisma, commercial potential or possession of a prototype. A prototype is
optional.

FRAMEWORK & PROCEDURE

Source basis & judge notes

Published hackathon criteria inform the domains below; the seven-category combination and equal weights are MSRC proposals, not a

HOW EACH MSRC DOMAIN WAS ADAPTED

01 Problem & Need

Harvard: Challenge | MIT Hacking Medicine: Impact
Defines the unmet problem, users and significance across both MSRC tracks.

02 Evidence & Scientific Rationale

Memphis: Evidence & Data Support | CMU: Validity
Separates scientific justification from persuasive delivery or projected impact.

03 Innovation & Solution Fit

Harvard: Technology & Innovation | MIT: Innovation
Makes technology optional and explicitly tests whether the idea addresses its users.

04 Feasibility & Implementation

Harvard: Implementation | Memphis: Feasibility & Implementation
Assesses implementation pathway and safeguards without requiring commercial revenue.

05 Impact & Sustainability

Memphis: Impact & Relevance | Scalability & Sustainability
Combines anticipated benefit with continuation; global scaling is not mandatory.

06 Pitch & Communication

Harvard: Pitch | Memphis: Presentation & Clarity | CMU: Presentation
Rewards concise explanation, not design polish or a working demo.

07 Q&A & Defense

Harvard: Question & Answer | CMU: Responses to questions
Rewards accurate, thoughtful defense, including acknowledgment of uncertainty.

JUDGING PROTOCOL

Panel: Score each finalist independently out of 35 within its assigned track. Judge conflicts should be declared and handled before scores
are combined.

Flag separately: Record major originality, ethics, privacy or safety concerns for committee review. A flag triggers review; any exclusion
requires a pre-agreed process.

Before the event: Confirm pitch timekeeping, treatment of absent presenters, procedures for technical failure, tie handling and any
proposed minimum passing thresholds.

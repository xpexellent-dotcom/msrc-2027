Executive overview

MSR Accelerate is a student innovation competition designed to translate research into useful healthcare solutions and to
reimagine the practice of medical and health research. It combines off-site preparation, structured mentoring during conference
Day 1, and competitive pitching and judging on Day 2. Participants may propose a solution without building a working prototype;
however, all finalists must present an evidence-supported, feasible implementation concept.

1. Purpose, scope and tracks

Purpose. Promote scientific reasoning, interdisciplinary collaboration, and practical innovation. The competition values both
learning and the development of useful research-informed ideas. A prototype or demonstration is encouraged, but is not
mandatory.

Working public-facing theme (proposed): "Accelerating Research-Driven Innovation." The name and theme are placeholders until
MSRC leadership approves branding.

Optional third track (conditional). An industry or pharmaceutical sponsor could provide a defined challenge. This track will only

2. Eligibility and team membership

   Eligible applicants: currently enrolled university students and interns studying in Saudi Arabia, from any academic discipline
     and of any nationality. No healthcare-specialty member is required; cross-university teams are permitted.

   Students may apply alone as a one-person team or in a pre-formed team of up to five. Automatic matching with other
     applicants is not offered.

   Each participant may belong to only one team. Each team may submit one project.
   The project lead creates the application and invites team members before the application deadline. Members accept

     invitations and complete the required hackathon account details during team formation. Conference registration may be
     completed separately after acceptance, subject to the published registration window and approval.
   The final team roster is confirmed by a published deadline. Changes after submission or acceptance require committee
     approval and may not be used to evade eligibility or team-size restrictions.
   Full team attendance is expected for mandatory event activities. At least one approved presenter must attend the final pitch;
     justified absences and their effect on eligibility or certificates require a documented committee decision, subject to
     leadership approval.

4. Applications and submissions

4.1 Initial application

Every application includes a project title, selected track and a single English-language pitch of no more than 300 words. The form
should guide applicants to cover all five elements below within that limit:

   Problem and target users or setting
   Relevant evidence and why the problem matters
   Proposed solution and how it works
   Novelty: how it differs from existing work
   Practical feasibility, expected impact and next steps
References are supplied in a separate field or attachment outside the 300-word count. Applicants may optionally attach one short
pitch deck, figures, supporting documents or links to a video/demonstration. Optional production quality should not replace the
core screening assessment. A pitch deck is a brief slide presentation outlining the problem, solution, evidence, innovation and
proposed implementation.

Account profiles contain necessary contact and enrollment details (name, email, phone, university and field of study). University
ID or equivalent proof of enrollment may be requested for verification, particularly after shortlisting. Collection of national
identity numbers, document images or other sensitive identifiers requires a justified purpose and institutional privacy approval.

Required declarations cover prior presentations and project history; AI use; team contributions; originality and permissions;
acknowledgment of competition rules; and appropriate media/data-processing consent. Research ethics or approval documents
are required only where applicable to activities actually undertaken.

4.2 Blinding and final-stage materials

All material seen by pre-event scientific reviewers must be anonymized. Proposal text, decks, filenames and visible metadata
must not contain applicant names, institutional logos, direct personal identifiers or other team-identifying details. A committee
eligibility officer retains access to identity and verification records separately.

Accepted finalists submit an English-language pitch deck with references and a sufficiently specific implementation approach. A
mockup, prototype, recording, screenshots or PDF walkthrough may be provided but is optional. A safe backup demonstration
may substitute for a failed live demo; it does not change the scoring standards. Spoken pitches and Q&A may be in Arabic or
English.

A convincing idea alone is not a sufficient final outcome: teams must show supporting evidence, identify the intended users or
setting, and explain practical feasibility and limitations. No functioning product or demonstrated clinical benefit is required.

5. Screening, decisions and reserve list

Step 1 -- Administrative and eligibility check. The registration lead verifies basic eligibility, one-person/one-team and one-project
limits, completion, project history declarations, prohibited content, and apparent ethics or safety concerns. Identity and
institutional proof should be limited to what is necessary and may be verified in stages.

Step 2 -- Blinded proposal review. Every administratively eligible application receives two independent scientific reviews using
the separately approved Screening Rubric. Reviewers score the proposed contribution and its distinction from existing work; they
do not need evidence that no comparable idea exists anywhere.

Step 3 -- Resolution and ranking. Material score disagreements, borderline shortlist decisions or disputed eligibility findings go to
a third independent reviewer or designated scientific officer; the hackathon lead oversees the process. The score-difference
trigger and final tie protocol must be fixed before screening. Reviewers declare conflicts and are reassigned when appropriate.

Step 4 -- Selection and confirmation. Up to eight teams per track are offered finalist places. Remaining eligible teams form a
track-specific ranked reserve list. Acceptance is provisional until eligibility, roster confirmation and separate conference
processes are completed. If an accepted team withdraws or is found ineligible, the committee may offer the place to the next
reserve team only if meaningful preparation and registration remain feasible.

Originality review. Applicants must disclose any earlier presentation, competition entry, prize, external funding,
commercialization or substantive development of the same project. A prior unawarded presentation may be reviewed case by
case; previously awarded, commercially launched or substantially completed projects are ineligible under the current working
rule. Funding and ambiguous prior development should be referred to the committee for a documented decision. Designated
eligibility staff may verify relevant public project records, published competition lists, LinkedIn posts and other public sources.
Similarity findings are not proof by themselves; teams must be given an opportunity to clarify.

6. Mentoring, presentations and live judging

6.1 Preparation and mentorship

Following acceptance, finalists join an online orientation and a WhatsApp support channel. The first conference day provides
structured, reasonably equitable access to mentors and dedicated team working time. Mentors may critique research evidence,
implementation pathways, clinical relevance, technical choices and presentation readiness; they may not take over a project,
create deliverables as undisclosed contributors or join a team in competition.

Mentors are coordinated through the Hackathon Lead and faculty/judges liaison. A short expert or sponsor workshop may be
included if space and time allow. Equal opportunity to consult mentors is required; identical advice or identical specialist
involvement is not.

6.2 Pitch and judges

Each finalist team receives five minutes to present and three minutes for judges' questions. Presentation order, timekeeper,
permissible number of speakers, overrun handling and audiovisual fallback will be released in the finalist instructions. Written
slides and supporting materials remain in English; the spoken presentation may be Arabic or English.

Ideally, each track has its own simultaneous panel of three to five judges with relevant clinical, scientific, research, technical and
implementation expertise. External university or industry judges may be invited subject to conference/KAU approval. Each judge
declares conflicts; scores are entered independently before a documented panel discussion. The separate Live Judging Rubric
governs assessment and must not be replaced by the application screening rubric.

Brief outcome notifications will be sent to applicants. Detailed individual reviewer scores, reviewer identities and confidential
panel notes are not routinely disclosed. Procedural complaints are handled through website support and committee escalation;
this does not create a general right to re-score substantive judging decisions.

7. Academic integrity, AI and responsible innovation

7.1 Generative AI policy

Generative AI is permitted for idea development, literature discovery, writing, coding, graphics, prototyping and preparing
presentations. Teams must disclose whether they used generative AI, identify significant tools/functions and remain able to
explain and defend their work. Disclosed use carries no automatic penalty or fixed percentage cap.

Teams must verify references and scientific claims. Fabricated sources, data or performance results; undisclosed copied work;
manipulated evidence; and the presentation of simulated or AI-generated outcomes as real validation are prohibited. Automated
AI-writing detectors are not used as a sole basis for disqualification. Significant integrity concerns are assessed by committee
members using available evidence and a chance for the team to respond.

7.2 Safety, ethics, conduct and ownership

Teams may use permitted public data, synthetic examples and suitably authorized de-identified data. Patient-identifying records
or confidential organizational material must not be uploaded to the platform or unapproved third-party AI services. Projects
involving actual participants, patient data, clinical testing or regulated activities must comply with applicable ethical, institutional
and data permissions before those activities occur. Preliminary safety flags are reviewed during application screening, with
specialist escalation when needed; live judges also assess safety and unsupported clinical claims.

Participants, mentors and judges must act respectfully and avoid harassment, falsification, plagiarism, sabotage and misuse of
confidential information. Serious conduct or integrity violations may lead to suspension or removal, following a documented
committee review.

As a working principle, teams retain rights in their own work, subject to applicable law, existing agreements and university
policies. MSRC obtains only the consent-based permissions necessary for review and agreed publicity. Public pitches are not
confidential; participants should not reveal unprotected sensitive information. There is no promise of patent protection,
adoption, incubation or commercialization.

Photo, video, team-image, pitch-recording and winner-description permissions must be covered by explicit registration terms
consistent with institutional policy. Full submitted proposals should not automatically be published. The committee and media
team need a defined process for no-photo requests, withdrawal requests and publicity corrections.

8. Registration, participant support, awards and partners

   Hackathon entry is free. Every finalist must still follow the regular MSRC conference registration, approval and any applicable
     conference access/payment rules. Hackathon acceptance does not itself grant conference admission.

   Pre-acceptance questions and complaints are submitted through the website support function. Platform notifications are
     sent by email in English. A support email should be verified and staffed before launch; the draft specifies
     MSRC27kau+hackathon@gmail.com as a candidate, not a confirmed address. Finalists additionally receive a managed
     WhatsApp support group.

   One winning team per track is planned, with a cash prize in each track. Prize amounts, source of funding, team distribution
     and payment approvals are pending conference leadership. Announcements must not promise unapproved amounts.

   Participation certificates are planned for finalists meeting published activity, final submission and feedback-survey
     requirements, with a documented process for justified absences. Winner, mentor and judge certificates are distinct. Final
     certificate criteria and signatories require approval.

   Potential contributors include KAU clinical departments, research groups, technology partners, healthcare organizations and
     innovation units. KAU's Center for Innovation and Entrepreneurship is a suggested outreach target for mentoring or a
     possible development pathway; no partnership or incubation outcome is confirmed.

   Out-of-city participants arrange and fund their own travel and accommodation unless leadership separately approves
     assistance.
